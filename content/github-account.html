A free account is all you need. The agent will do the rest of the GitHub work once it's connected to your account.

An agent can't sign up for you: the account belongs to you and is tied to your email address.

  1. Sign up

    Go to https://github.com/signup and follow the prompts. You can also sign up with your Google or Apple account.

  2. Choose a short username

    Your username becomes part of your site's address: https://your-username.github.io/. Keep it short and lowercase, since you'll share it in every link. Changing it later breaks old links, so pick one you'll keep.

  3. Verify your email

    GitHub asks you to verify your email address during sign-up. Do it right away. Until you do, you can't create or fork a repository, which this guide needs.

  4. Turn on two-factor authentication

    Recommended. It stops anyone who learns your password from getting in. Click your profile picture, then Settings → Password and authentication → Enable two-factor authentication. Use an authenticator app on your phone. When GitHub shows your recovery codes, download them and keep them somewhere safe.

  5. Keep your email private and copy your noreply address

    Click your profile picture, then Settings → Emails. Tick Keep my email addresses private. GitHub then shows a private address that looks like this:

    ID+your-username@users.noreply.github.com

    Copy it. You'll give it to the agent so your commits are credited to you, without putting your real email in your site's public history. Commit as yourself shows how.

  6. Save your details in this guide

    Save your details here once; every prompt in this guide fills them in.

Open https://github.com/your-username. You see your profile page. Your noreply address is copied somewhere you can find it again.

If it goes wrong

The username I want is taken

Add a number or a hyphen, or try another spelling. Keep it lowercase.

GitHub won't let me create or fork a repository

Your email probably isn't verified yet. Open Settings → Emails and click Resend verification email under your address. Then click the link in the email GitHub sends. Check your spam folder if it doesn't arrive. Throwaway email addresses can't be verified, so use a real one.
