Three places your project lives

  1. Your repository on GitHub. The official copy of your site's files, plus the history of every saved change. It lives at github.com/your-username/your-repo.
  2. The working copy. The copy the agent actually edits. On the VS Code path it's a folder on your computer. On the app path it's a fresh copy in the cloud, made for each session. Nothing here reaches GitHub until it's pushed.
  3. The live website. GitHub Pages builds it from your repository and serves it at https://your-username.github.io/your-repo/. Changes can take up to 10 minutes to show after a push.

Four words move changes between them. You'll rarely type them yourself; you'll ask the agent.

WordWhat it does
CloneMakes a working copy of a repository.
PullBrings the working copy up to date with GitHub.
CommitSaves a snapshot of your changes, with a short note.
PushSends your commits up to GitHub.

How the agent works

  1. You ask

    Type or say what you want in plain words, and name the repo it should work in.

  2. It reads and plans

    It reads the files in the working copy and works out what to change. On a big job, ask to see the plan before it starts.

  3. It does the work

    It edits files and runs commands in that working copy. It may stop and ask permission before a command or a risky step. Read the request before you approve it.

  4. You check, then save

    Look at what changed. When you're happy, tell it to save the work to GitHub:

    Commit and push to main.

    Once GitHub Pages is on, your live site updates too. Commit & push covers this step in detail.

What it can see, and what it remembers

  • Only what you give it. It works in the folder you opened in VS Code, or the repos you picked in the app. It doesn't see the rest of your computer unless you allow it.
  • Each session starts fresh. It doesn't carry your earlier conversations into a new session. What carries over is the repo: its files, its history and a rules file. Claude reads CLAUDE.md at the start of every session, and Codex reads AGENTS.md. Put standing rules there, such as who to commit as or "never invent facts". Commit as yourself shows one.
You can answer two questions: where does the agent make its edits (the working copy), and what makes them show up on your site (commit and push, then Pages publishes)?
