The one rule

You type or say what you want. The agent does the work. You check it.

Most pages give you a Say this prompt to copy or dictate to the agent. You do things by hand only where the agent has no access: creating your GitHub account, signing in, approving a permission request, installing VS Code if you take that path, and changing a setting on github.com. The main one is turning on GitHub Pages, once, in your repo's Settings → Pages.

A prompt looks like this
Change the site title to "A History of St. Petersburg" and show me what you changed.

What you'll end with

Your own site at https://your-username.github.io/your-repo/, on a subject you choose. You start by copying one of two real sites:

  • history-of-tampa, one long scrolling story. Use it to learn any history subject: a city or neighborhood, a material like indigo, an industry, a company or a movement. Researching, sourcing and telling the story is how you learn it.
  • startup-india-guide, a multi-page minisite with a sidebar, like this guide. Use it to organize any subject that needs several pages: a directory, a handbook, a resource guide or a program catalog.

The running example turns history-of-tampa into a history of St. Petersburg, Florida, in a repo called history-of-st-pete. All four sites below are live.

The five steps

Step 2 is the only fork in the road: the app path or the VS Code path. Both lead to the same published site.

The VS Code path works on your computer. Step 3 · Just enough GitRepos, forks, commits and pushesThe few words you need, and the prompts that do the work. Step 4 · Make it yoursPick a starter and rewrite itCopy history-of-tampa or startup-india-guide, then tell the agent your subject. Step 5 · PublishTurn on GitHub PagesOne setting you switch on yourself. Then your site is live.

What you need

  • A GitHub account. It's free. Step 1 walks you through it.
  • A paid AI plan. Claude Pro or higher for Claude Code, or ChatGPT Plus or higher for Codex. The free Claude plan doesn't include Claude Code.
  • A phone or a computer. The app path works from a phone or a browser. The VS Code path needs a computer.
