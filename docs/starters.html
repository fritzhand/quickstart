Step 4 · Make it yours

Pick a starter

Don't start from a blank page. Copy a real, working site and have the agent rewrite it for your subject. One starter tells a history as a long scrolling story. The other organizes any subject into a multi-page guide with a sidebar.

Time: 5 min

The two starters

Starter · learn a history · easiesthistory-of-tampaOne long scrolling story of 200 years of downtown Tampa, with a map, charts and archival photos. Use it to learn any history: a city or neighborhood, a material like indigo, an industry, a company or a movement. Plain files, no build step. (opens in a new tab) Starter · build a minisite · harderstartup-india-guideA multi-page minisite with a sidebar and search, built from data files. Use it to organize any subject that needs several pages: a directory, a handbook, a resource guide or a program catalog. (opens in a new tab)

First time? Pick history-of-tampa. This guide's running example turns it into a history of St. Petersburg, Florida, but the same steps work for any history subject. Building a directory or guide instead? Pick startup-india-guide.

history-of-indigo was built from a single message. Make your own version shows that prompt and how to use it for your own subject.

Want a sidebar guide like the one you're reading? This guide is built the same way as startup-india-guide and published from main, /docs. Its repo is fritzhand/quickstart (opens in a new tab).

Look before you choose

Have the agent walk you through a starter before you copy it. On the app path, add the starter to a session as a reference repo (Many repos, one to work in).

Say this
Look at fritzhand/history-of-tampa and tell me, in plain English, which files I'd change to make a history of my subject, and what I should delete first.

For the other starter, swap in fritzhand/startup-india-guide and "a guide to" instead of "a history of".

Licensing in plain words

You may reuse both starters. Each comes with a few conditions.

history-of-tampa (and history-of-indigo)

  • Code: CC0. Free to reuse for anything, no credit required.
  • Compiled data in js/data.js: CC BY 4.0. Reuse it only with credit. You'll replace it with your own research anyway.
  • Author photo (assets/jeremy.jpg): all rights reserved. Delete it.
  • Images the site shows: each carries its own license, listed in CONTENT_LICENSE.md. Keep the credit line for any image you keep.

startup-india-guide

  • Code: MIT. Free to reuse and change, but keep the LICENSE file with its copyright notice.
  • Author photo (site/jeremy.png): a photo of the original author. Delete it.

Always keep a credit

Keep a line such as "Forked from fritzhand/history-of-tampa", with a link, in your site's footer and README. It's a courtesy for the code, and it's how you credit any data or images you keep.

Check it worked

You know which starter you'll use and which Pages folder it publishes from: / (root) for history-of-tampa, /docs for startup-india-guide. Next: Make your own version.
